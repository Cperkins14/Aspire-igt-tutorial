2
Install Ollama on your Mac
On your own   About 10 minutes

Ollama is a free app that lets you run AI directly on your Mac. No wifi needed once it's set up, no accounts, completely free. This is what we're going to use to run your simulation.

  • 1 Go to ollama.com in your browser
  • 2 Click the Download button — it'll automatically know you're on a Mac
  • 3 Open the downloaded file and drag Ollama into your Applications folder, same as any other app
  • 4 Open Ollama from your Applications folder. You'll see a little llama icon show up in your menu bar at the top right — that means it's running and you're good
If your Mac blocks it
If you get a popup saying it's from an unidentified developer, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to click "Open Anyway." This is totally normal for apps you download outside the App Store.
3
Download the AI model
On your own   5–15 minutes depending on your wifi

Now you need to actually download the AI we're using. It's called Llama 3 and it's about 4GB, so make sure you're on wifi before you start this.

Open the Terminal app on your Mac. Press Cmd + Space, type "Terminal", and hit Enter. It'll open a black window with a blinking cursor — don't stress, you're only typing one thing.

Terminal is just a black window with a cursor. You're literally only typing one line so don't overthink it.

Type this and press Enter:

ollama pull llama3

You'll see it downloading with a progress bar. When it says "success" you're done — close Terminal.

What just happened
You downloaded a 4GB AI model straight onto your Mac. It runs completely offline from here — no data goes anywhere, it's free to use as many times as you want.
4
Make sure it actually works
On your own   2 minutes

Open Terminal again and run this:

ollama run llama3 "Say hello in one sentence"

If it responds with something like "Hello! It's great to meet you." then it's working. Screenshot it and send it to me on Discord so I know you're all set before we meet.

If you get an error
Don't stress — just screenshot whatever it says and send it to me on Discord before the meeting. The most common thing is that Ollama isn't open. Check your menu bar for the llama icon and click it to open it, then try again.
5
Find clinical descriptions of bipolar episodes
On your own   1–2 hours of reading

This is honestly the most important thing to do this week. The whole simulation depends on the patient profiles you build, and those need to come from real published sources — not anything AI-generated.

You're looking for descriptions of what manic and depressive episodes actually look like — symptoms, behaviors, how the person thinks and acts. Here's where to look:

  • 1 DSM-5 — just Google "DSM-5 Bipolar I manic episode criteria" — the official descriptions are really detailed and exactly what you need
  • 2 Google Scholar — search "bipolar I manic episode case study" or "bipolar depressive episode clinical description"
  • 3 Your Iowa Gambling Task paper — look through its reference list, some of those papers probably describe bipolar patients in detail. Click into them.
  • 4 nimh.nih.gov and nami.org — both have solid research-backed descriptions of bipolar episodes
Save everything you find
For each source — write down the full citation, copy the part that stood out to you, and one sentence on why it's useful. You'll be plugging these descriptions directly into the simulation.
Paper behind a paywall?
Don't pay for it. Just save the citation and send it to me — I can get it for you.
